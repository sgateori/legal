TorHostnet Privacy Policy

App: TorHostnet (net.torhostnet) · Developer: sgateori · Last updated: 2026-10-22

TorHostnet serves a folder you choose from your phone as a Tor onion service. It has no accounts, shows no ads and sells no data. The developer has no server in the path and cannot see your site. This page says what stays on your phone and what leaves it.

What stays on your device

Your site files, your onion address and your onion private key stay on your phone. Site files are read from the folder you pick (through Android's folder picker) or from the app's own site folder, and handed to the Tor program on request. The onion key is kept in the app's storage and is excluded from Android cloud backup and device transfer. If you create an identity backup file, it is encrypted with a key derived from your password and stays where you save it. Uninstalling the app removes its stored data.

Who can reach your site

Visitors reach your site through Tor. Anyone who knows your .onion address can fetch your site; the app sets up no visitor authorisation. The developer has no copy of your files and cannot see or remove what you publish. The built-in web server listens only on your phone (127.0.0.1), so other devices on your Wi-Fi cannot reach it directly.

Network connections

The embedded Tor program connects out to the Tor network (to find relays, to publish your onion service descriptor and to carry visitors' requests). The developer does not run these servers. Tor is a third-party network; see torproject.org. The app does not use Android's VPN service. If you opt in to crash reports (below), that upload goes directly to Google and does not use Tor.

Crash reports and anonymous usage (Firebase)

This is off by default. Nothing is collected on first launch. If you turn on the switch Share crash reports and usage statistics in the app, it uses Google Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase Analytics, with Google as a service provider processing the data for us. Turning it off stops further collection. It is always off in debug builds.

The app deliberately does not send your onion address, onion key, site files or content, folder name, request paths, visitor counts or Tor log lines, and it does not use the advertising ID.

Limit: if the app crashes with an uncaught error, the error text written by the system or a library is sent as it is. The app removes the message from the handled problems it reports itself, but not from uncaught crashes. In rare cases such text might include a folder address or file path from a failed read, or Tor error text. This only applies after you have turned the switch on.

Because this upload does not go through Tor, Google sees your phone's real IP address, that it runs TorHostnet, and when, and may derive an approximate country or region from the IP address. The app itself never reads your location.

Data is sent over HTTPS. Retention is set by Google's defaults for Firebase. See Firebase privacy information and the Google Privacy Policy.

What we do not collect

We do not collect your onion address or key, your site, its visitors, your location, contacts, files, advertising ID, or any account or personal information. We do not sell data and do not use it for advertising.

Permissions

Internet (Tor, and Firebase if you opt in); network state (notice network and VPN changes and reconnect); notifications (the hosting notification, with a Shut down action); a foreground service of the special-use type and a wake lock (keep your site online while hosting is on, even after you swipe the app away, until you shut it down). No storage, location, contacts, camera or microphone access, and no all-files access.

Your choices and deleting data

Turn the Firebase switch off at any time. Stop hosting from the notification or the app. Delete data on your phone in the app or by uninstalling. We have no account system, so we cannot look up your data by name. To ask us to delete reports tied to your Firebase installation ID, email us that ID or a description of when a crash happened, and we will delete what we can match.

Children

TorHostnet is a general-purpose tool and is not directed at children.

Changes

If this changes, the date above changes and the app update notes will say so.
